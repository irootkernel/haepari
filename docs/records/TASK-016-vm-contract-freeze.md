# TASK-016 VM contract freeze decision

Decision date: 2026-10-05; qualification and recovery candidate updated 2026-10-08. Effective when TASK-016 becomes Accepted in the [canonical roadmap](../roadmap.md). This record defines the candidate F2 boundary; it does not independently accept the Task or Epic. The accepted Task commit and its review/verification evidence identify the exact frozen bytes.

## Scope and authorities

F2 freezes the implemented `utm-macos` resource fields, authenticated guest request/reply and worker protocols, VM interpretation of native run identity, host-session ownership, and VM-specific Job/result behavior. Semantic owners remain [resource/runner](../contracts/resource-and-runner.md), [CLI/jobs](../contracts/cli-and-jobs.md), [managed transitions](../contracts/managed-transitions.md), and [configuration](../contracts/configuration.md). The accepted code's typed contracts and validators are the complete field inventory; the table below identifies those owners rather than introducing a second schema.

The [F1 decision](TASK-012-managed-core-freeze.md) continues to own common admission, local execution, native runner receipts, outcomes, artifact delivery and evidence-recovery schemas. F2 specializes those existing bindings for a separate guest filesystem; it changes neither F1 local execution nor storage schema 9. Manual completion, environment locks, consumer installation conventions and the general configuration loader remain with their later tasks. Public external leases remain unsupported.

## Guest-attested recovery candidate boundary

The explicit guest-attested evidence recovery capability adds a separate recovery protocol and durable operator-provenance/retirement path for legacy retained false-proof Jobs. It preserves the original guest/request/exit/control contracts and terminal Job verdict; the original frozen runner is not replaced or supplied a stronger termination receipt. Required outputs, native log and original receipts must be copied and verified before any owned guest cleanup, followed by a separately planned health observation. See [resource and runner contract](../contracts/resource-and-runner.md#explicit-guest-attested-evidence-recovery) and [operating sequence](../operations.md#recover-retained-guest-evidence-with-a-scoped-attestation).

The reviewed recovery candidate has now been installed in the same isolated development service under separately approved live authority. The retained legacy Job completed scoped guest attestation, verified recollection of its required output, native log and six original JSON receipts, managed removal of its exact owned guest Job root, and separately planned resource health. The original terminal INCOMPLETE result and native false termination proof remain unchanged. The legacy guest runner was not replaced. These observations prove the recovery path, not a fresh workload qualification.

The first submission timed out while the locked host daemon waited in native `openat`. After Master unlocked the host, the wait cleared and no Job had been admitted. An explicitly approved continuation window replayed the exact saved key and Job specification. The installed recovery candidate then passed normal execution, post-cleanup reuse, intentional workload failure, semantic cancellation, actual SSH observer disconnection with original-run reconciliation, and isolated controller restart/caller disconnection with original-run recovery. Native request/exit identity and transferred runner digest, collected artifacts, guest-root absence, once markers and capacity were verified. The continuity helper initially reused a health mutation key for two different plans; the native controller correctly rejected the second input as CONFLICT. A distinct saved key completed the second separately planned health operation without rerunning either workload or rewriting its terminal result. Original helper failure evidence remains retained.

The first screen-lock Job completed PASS, but native console sampling observed an unlock during the reservation-through-cleanup interval. That attempt remains partial lock evidence. A fresh missing-required-output Job completed its workload but retained the exact guest root and settled INCOMPLETE/partial with failed cleanup; its successor remained queued without a lease and was cancelled. After separate exact-loss approval, a fresh native plan disposed only that retained Job's owned guest root. A separately planned health operation restored availability. The last already-budgeted reuse Job then completed PASS with complete collection, verified guest-root absence, a once-only workload marker and available capacity. Native console sampling observed the same logged-in session locked throughout reservation through cleanup, with 32 samples at approximately half-second intervals. A preceding lock-wait attempt ended without admitting any Job; its failed record is preserved. The successful attempt used the same frozen helper and original approved disposal/reuse deadline. Across the continuation, nine executed admissions and one queued successor consumed the original aggregate budget; no extra Job, time extension or review-budget reset was introduced. A disposal observer initially read the receipt's job stage from the wrong field; the native completed receipt was reconciled without repeating disposal. Both helper errors and their corrections remain separate from product outcomes.
